What Google Looks For (Restaurant-Specific)

Google's local ranking factors for restaurants are specific. Here's what matters most:

Google Business Profile completeness. Every field filled out. Every category selected. Your hours, your menu link, your attributes (outdoor seating, delivery, takeout, etc.). Google rewards profiles that give users complete information.

Reviews — quantity, quality, and recency. A restaurant with 87 reviews averaging 4.6 stars will outrank one with 12 reviews at 4.9 stars. Google wants to see consistent review activity, not a burst from two years ago. Responding to every review (positive and negative) also signals engagement.

Photos. Restaurants with 100+ photos on their GBP get significantly more engagement. Interior shots, exterior shots, food photos, team photos. Google tracks how users interact with your photos, and that data feeds into rankings.

Local citations. Your restaurant's name, address, and phone number need to match exactly across Yelp, TripAdvisor, OpenTable, Zomato, Apple Maps, Facebook, and dozens of other directories. Inconsistencies confuse Google and hurt your rankings.

On-page SEO on your website. Title tags with "restaurant in Palm Coast, FL," meta descriptions that include your cuisine type, schema markup for restaurants (menu, hours, reviews), and content that naturally references your location.

The Palm Coast Restaurant SEO Checklist

Here's what to do this week:

1. Audit your Google Business Profile. Log in and check every field. Is your primary category "Restaurant"? Did you add secondary categories like "Seafood Restaurant" or "Italian Restaurant"? Are your hours accurate, including holiday hours?

2. Add 20+ photos this month. Mix of food, interior, exterior, and staff. Upload them directly to your GBP — don't just post them on social media.

3. Fix your citations. Search your restaurant name on Google. Check Yelp, TripAdvisor, and Facebook. Make sure your name, address, and phone number are identical everywhere. Even small differences (St. vs. Street, Suite vs. Ste.) can cause problems.

4. Start a review request system. After every meal, send a text or email asking for a review. Make it easy — include a direct link to your Google review page. Aim for 5-10 new reviews per month.

5. Post weekly on your GBP. Share specials, new menu items, events, and behind-the-scenes content. Use local keywords naturally. Google sees active profiles as more relevant.

6. Add schema markup to your website. Restaurant schema tells Google exactly what you serve, where you are, your hours, and your menu. This helps you qualify for rich results in search.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take for a restaurant to rank on Google Maps in Palm Coast? Most restaurants see meaningful ranking improvements within 60-90 days of consistent local SEO work. Some categories move faster than others — if you're the only Thai restaurant in the area, you'll rank faster than if you're one of 15 pizza places.

Do I need a website for local SEO? You can rank on Google Maps without a website, but having one significantly helps. Your website gives Google more content to index, more signals to evaluate, and more ways for customers to find you. At minimum, make sure your site is mobile-friendly and loads fast.

What's more important — reviews or citations? Both matter, but reviews have a more direct impact on rankings. Citations build authority over time, while reviews provide immediate social proof and ranking signals. You need both working together.
