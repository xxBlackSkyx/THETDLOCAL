# Hurricane Season Marketing For Florida Contractors

Hurricane season marketing for Florida contractors is the practice of adjusting your online presence around the storm calendar so customers find you when they need repairs, cleanup, or emergency service. If you run a roofing, plumbing, electrical, tree, or landscaping business in Flagler County, your phone rings hardest in the days after a storm, and your competitors are already fighting for that call. The contractors who win are the ones whose Google Business Profile, service pages, and reviews are ready before the wind picks up. Today is October 15, and the season runs through the end of November, so there is still time to make changes that matter.

## What Hurricane Season Marketing Means for Florida Contractors

Hurricane season marketing is not a one-time post about hurricane preparedness. It is a set of changes to your local search presence that match the way homeowners behave when a storm threatens. Before a storm, people search for generators, tarps, tree removal, and gutter repair. After a storm, they search for "emergency roof repair near me," "water heater leak today," or "fallen tree removal Palm Coast." Each of those searches is a local query, and Google decides which contractors to show based on relevance, distance, and prominence.

For a Florida contractor, that means three things. First, your Google Business Profile must list the services you actually perform, not just your primary trade. A plumber near Town Center who also handles sewer line jetting and water heater replacement should list each one as a separate service. Second, your website needs pages that match those services, with the city named in plain language. Third, your reviews need to reflect recent work, because storm-season customers read reviews closely and often choose based on the most recent few.

Hurricane season marketing also means planning for disruption. Your office may lose power. Your phone may get flooded with calls you cannot answer. Your crew may be booked out for weeks. A good plan addresses how your profile communicates availability, how you respond to inquiries, and how you set expectations so a homeowner does not leave a bad review because they waited. This is the difference between a storm that brings you a surge of profitable jobs and one that brings you a surge of angry messages.

## Step-by-Step Hurricane Season Marketing Plan

Start with your Google Business Profile, because it drives most of the local calls for contractors. Log in and open the Services section. Remove any service you no longer offer, then add every storm-related service you perform, such as emergency roof tarping, tree removal, water extraction referrals if that applies to your license, generator hookups, gutter repair, and fence repair. Write each service name the way a homeowner would search it. "Emergency Roof Repair" is clearer than "Roof Services Plus."

Next, check your Service Area settings. If you serve Palm Coast, Flagler Beach, Bunnell, and Matanzas Woods, list each city. If you only work within a set radius, be honest about it. A roofer who lists areas they cannot reach will get calls they cannot serve, and that damages reviews. Then open Business Hours and set special hours for storm weeks. If your office will be closed, mark it closed on specific dates instead of leaving stale hours that confuse people. Use the "Temporarily closed" status only when you truly cannot take work, because removing it quickly after the storm is just as important.

Third, update your business description and add a Google Post. Keep the description factual: the cities you serve, your licensed trades, your emergency contact method, and whether you offer 24-hour service. Post a short update that says what homeowners should do if they have water intrusion or a damaged roof, and include your phone number. Posts appear in your profile and remain visible for a limited period, so refresh them every week or two during storm season.

Fourth, review your website. Each service you list on your profile needs a matching page or section on your site, with the city named in the heading and body. A plumber with a page titled "Emergency Plumbing Palm Coast" will align better than one with a generic home page. Our [Seo For Plumbers Palm Coast Fl](https://tdlocalseo.com/blog/seo-for-plumbers-palm-coast-fl) resource walks through how to structure those pages so search engines can match them to local queries.

Fifth, ask for reviews before the storm, not after. Send a short text or email to recent customers asking for a review of their most recent job. Include a direct link to your review form from your Google Business Profile dashboard. Reviews written during the calm weeks give your profile strength when the surge arrives.

Sixth, prepare your response plan. Write three short templates: one for after-hours calls, one for homeowners on a waitlist, and one for review replies. A quick, polite reply to a waitlist message reduces frustration and keeps the customer engaged. Finally, check your ads and call tracking. If you run paid campaigns, pause or adjust them when crews are fully booked, so you are not paying for leads you cannot serve.

## Common Hurricane Season Marketing Mistakes

The most common mistake is waiting until a storm is forecast to update anything. By then, search demand is rising, and Google's systems are already processing profile changes from every competitor in your area. Make your updates in the calm weeks, then monitor them during the storm.

The second mistake is listing too many cities without the work to back them up. A contractor who claims to serve every community in Flagler County but only shows reviews from one neighborhood will struggle to rank for the others. Focus on the areas where you have completed jobs, and add more as your review base grows.

The third mistake is ignoring the profile when the office is closed. If your hours still say "Open" on a day you cannot answer calls, customers will call, wait, and move on. Set accurate special hours and post a message that tells people how to reach you.

The fourth mistake is neglecting photos. Storm damage work is visual, and homeowners want to see that you have handled similar situations. Upload before-and-after photos of completed repairs, with the neighborhood name if the customer has agreed. Avoid stock photos of storms, which do nothing for your credibility.

The fifth mistake is treating reviews as a reputation problem instead of a ranking signal. Respond to every review, positive or negative, within a few days. Negative reviews written during a storm rush are common, so a calm, specific reply that explains what happened and what you did about it protects your profile. Ignoring them makes the profile look abandoned.

The sixth mistake is running a website with no storm-related content. If your site has no page about emergency roof tarping, tree removal after wind events, or water damage repair, you are asking Google to guess what you do. Build those pages before the season, and keep them accurate.

## Local FAQ: Hurricane Season Marketing for Florida Contractors

### How early should a Palm Coast contractor start hurricane season marketing?

Start in the calm weeks before a storm threatens, ideally now, with October still in the season. Update your Google Business Profile services, build or refresh your service pages, and request reviews from recent customers. Waiting until a storm is forecast gives you little time, because search demand climbs quickly and competitors are already updating their listings. Early work also gives Google time to process your changes, so your profile is stable when the calls begin. Plan to refresh posts and hours weekly during the active months.

### Should I pause paid ads during a storm?

Pause or adjust paid ads when your crews are fully booked or your office cannot take calls. Paying for clicks that lead to voicemail wastes budget and frustrates homeowners who may then leave negative reviews. Keep a small budget running if you can answer calls and handle the demand, and increase it when you have capacity. Set your ad schedule to match your real working hours, and turn on call tracking so you can see which campaigns produce answered calls rather than just clicks.

### What should my Google Business Profile say during a storm?

Your profile should state your services, service areas, emergency contact method, and current availability in plain language. Use a Google Post to explain what homeowners should do if they have a roof leak, water intrusion, or fallen tree, and include your phone number. If you are temporarily closed, set that status. If you are open with a waitlist, say so in the description and post. Avoid vague messages like "call for details" because they do not help a homeowner decide quickly. Clear, factual updates reduce wasted calls and improve trust.

### How do I get reviews from storm customers without seeming pushy?

Ask within a few days of completing the job, when the customer remembers the work clearly. Send a short message that thanks them, names the specific repair, and includes a direct link to your review form. Avoid offering incentives, since that can violate Google's review policies. If a customer had a difficult experience, respond to their concern first and invite them to share feedback later. A steady flow of recent, specific reviews from ordinary jobs is more useful than a sudden spike from a single storm week.
