You're a small business owner in Palm Coast. You've got a website, maybe a Google Business Profile, and you're showing up... somewhere on page two. Sound familiar?

## What a Local SEO Audit Actually Does for Your Business

A local SEO audit is a systematic review of everything Google uses to decide whether to show your business when someone searches locally. It covers your website, your Google Business Profile, your citations, your reviews, competitive benchmarks, and technical health.

The goal isn't to make things look pretty. The goal is to find the specific problems keeping you from the top 3 in Google Maps — the spot that gets the majority of clicks.

In a market like Palm Coast, where many competitors neglect basic SEO, a thorough audit can reveal quick wins that put you ahead within weeks, not months.

## Google Business Profile: The Foundation of Local Search

Over 80% of local searches result in a Google Maps Pack — those three business listings at the top of results. If your GBP isn't dialed in, nothing else matters as much.

Check these items:

- **Business name matches your real-world name exactly** — no stuffing keywords like "Palm Coast Best Plumber" if that's not your legal business name
- **Primary category is the most specific match** — "HVAC Contractor" not just "Contractor" if that's what you do
- **All applicable secondary categories are added** — up to 9 additional categories that describe your services
- **Service area is set correctly** — Palm Coast, Flagler Beach, Bunnell, Flagler areas you actually serve
- **Phone number is a local area code** — 386 for Flagler County
- **Website URL points to the most relevant page** — deep link to your services page, not just the homepage when possible
- **Hours are accurate and up to date** — including holiday hours if applicable
- **Business description is complete** — 750 characters, keyword-rich but readable, mentions Palm Coast and services
- **Attributes are filled out** — wheelchair accessible, women-owned, veteran-owned, etc. if applicable

**Palm Coast specific flag:** Many Google Business Profiles in our area list only "Palm Coast" as the service area. If you also serve Flagler Beach, Daytona Beach outskirts, or Bunnell, add those. Google rewards businesses that are transparent about where they serve.

## Consistency of Your Business Information Online

Google cross-references your business data across the web. Inconsistencies — even small ones — erode trust and tank rankings.

Run through these directories and verify every listing matches exactly:
- Your business name (no abbreviations on one site and full name on another)
- Your address (suite number format, street abbreviation — pick one and stick with it)
- Your phone number
- Your website URL

Key directories to check first: Google Business Profile, Yelp, Facebook Business Page, Bing Places, Apple Maps, and the Flagler County Chamber of Commerce directory.

You don't need to be on every directory on earth. You need to be consistent on the ones that matter.

## On-Page SEO: What's Actually on Your Website

This is where most Palm Coast businesses fall apart. Their websites were built years ago, nobody's touched the content, and pages are thin — which tells Google there's not enough substance to rank.

Audit each service page and your homepage for these elements:

**Title tags:** Every page should have a unique title under 60 characters that includes your target keyword. "Local HVAC Services in Palm Coast FL | [Business Name]" not just "Home."

**H1 headings:** One per page, includes the primary keyword for that page naturally.

**Content depth:** At least 500 words of original content per service page. If your roofing page has three sentences and a phone call to action, that's not enough.

**Local mentions:** Your content should reference specific neighborhoods, landmarks, areas, and nearby cities. Palm Harbor, Town Center, Old Belle Terre, the Hammock, I-95 corridor — these local signals tell Google you're genuinely rooted in Flagler County.

**Internal links:** Service pages should link to each other and to related blog posts. Every supporting blog post should link back to your pillar page — your main Local SEO services page. This passes authority and clarifies your site structure for Google.

**One call to action per page:** Tell visitors what to do — call now, request a free quote, book online. Don't bury it.

## Technical Website Health

Google can't rank what it can't crawl. Run these checks:

- **Mobile-friendly?** Over 60% of local searches happen on phones. Open your site on a phone right now. Buttons easy to tap? Text readable without zooming? Fast load time?
- **Page speed:** Use Google PageSpeed Insights. Your site should load in under 3 seconds. Vercel-hosted sites, like tdlocalseo.com, typically excel here. If you're on cheap shared hosting, that's likely your bottleneck.
- **SSL certificate:** Your URL should start with https://. No exceptions in 2026.
- **No broken links:** A tool like Screaming Frog or even a free online checker can find 404 errors. Every broken link wastes crawl budget.
- **Structured data markup:** Add LocalBusiness schema to your homepage. This tells Google your business name, address, phone, hours, and services in a machine-readable format.

## How Reviews Impact Your Local Rankings

Reviews are one of the top 3 local ranking factors. In a market like Palm Coast with a manageable number of competitors, review velocity and quality can push you to the top faster than almost anything else.

Audit your review profile:
- **Total review count** — how do you stack up against your top 3 competitors?
- **Average star rating** — below 4.0 is a red flag for both Google and customers
- **Review response rate** — have you replied to every review, positive and negative?
- **Recency** — when was your last review? One per month is the minimum to signal an active business
- **Keyword relevance** — do reviewers naturally mention your services and location? ("Great roofing company in Palm Coast" is gold for rankings)

If you're behind on reviews, fix that. Ask every happy customer. Make it easy — send a direct Google review link via text after every job.

## What Your Competitors Are Doing (And How to Beat Them)

Find the three businesses ranking above you for your primary keyword on Google Maps. Study what they're doing:

- How many reviews do they have?
- How complete is their GBP?
- Do they post on their GBP weekly?
- What does their website look like?
- How many backlinks point to their site?

You don't need to copy them. You need to do what they do well and fix what they're doing wrong. In Palm Coast, most competitors are neglecting at least 2-3 of these areas.

## FAQ

**How often should I run a local SEO audit?**
Quarterly at minimum. Google updates its local algorithm regularly, and your competitors are (hopefully) improving too. A quick monthly check on reviews and GBP plus a deeper quarterly audit keeps you ahead.

**Can I do a local SEO audit myself?**
You can catch the obvious issues — missing GBP info, no reviews, broken site elements. For technical SEO, competitive analysis, and citation cleanup, it's worth bringing in a professional who knows the Palm Coast market.

**How long before I see results after fixing audit issues?**
GBP fixes can impact rankings within 2-4 weeks. Website changes typically take 4-8 days to be re-crawled. Content improvements and backlink building are longer-term plays — expect 2-3 months for full effect.
